At least two writers should ideally be Caribbean students aged 16–18. The
remaining writers may be adults. Use no names, school names, candidate numbers,
faces or other identifying information.

Writing rules:

- Use ordinary ruled paper and an ordinary blue or black pen. Pencil is also
  acceptable where that is how the writer normally does mathematics.
- Copy the mathematics faithfully, including every deliberate wrong value.
  Do not correct it and do not add missing steps.
- Write normal handwritten symbols: `×`, `√`, `π`, fractions and powers are
  preferable to literally spelling `x`, `sqrt` or `pi`.
- Put the part label beside the first line of that part. Later lines may inherit
  the label, as they would on a real script.
- Natural spacing, overwriting and a small correction are fine. Do not
  deliberately disguise handwriting or make the photograph artificially bad.
- Take one normal phone photograph of the whole page. A slight angle, ordinary
  indoor light or a mild shadow is realistic; blur, cropped work and filters are
  not useful test data.
